A home maintenance checklist by month and season

Houses rarely fail all at once. A gutter overflows for two winters and the fascia rots. A filter goes unchanged and the furnace works harder every month. Most of the expensive repairs started as a small job nobody wrote down. A routine you can see on paper fixes that, and it takes less time than you would guess.

This is a general checklist for a typical house. Your manufacturers' instructions come first, and gas, electrical and roof work belong to a licensed pro.

Every month

  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
  • Check the HVAC filter and replace it if it is dirty (many need changing every one to three months)
  • Run water in guest bathrooms and unused drains so the traps do not dry out
  • Look under sinks and around the water heater for drips or damp spots
  • Clean the garbage disposal and the range hood filter

Spring

  • Clear the gutters and check that downspouts carry water away from the foundation
  • Look over the roof from the ground for missing or lifted shingles
  • Service the air conditioner before the first hot week
  • Check the caulk and screens on windows, and the weatherstripping on doors
  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit

Summer

  • Clean the dryer vent and the lint trap housing
  • Inspect decks and fences for loose boards and soft spots
  • Check outdoor faucets and hoses for leaks
  • Trim branches that touch the roof or siding

Fall

  • Clean the gutters again after the leaves drop
  • Shut off and drain outdoor faucets before the first freeze
  • Have the furnace checked, and sweep the chimney if you burn wood
  • Seal gaps around doors and windows
  • Replace smoke alarm batteries

Winter

  • Know where your main water shutoff is, and test it
  • Check for ice dams and icicles at the roof edge
  • Keep the area around the furnace and water heater clear
  • Watch for condensation on windows, a sign of excess humidity

The long-cycle jobs

Some jobs come around every few years, so they get forgotten: flushing the water heater, replacing smoke alarms at ten years, a septic pump-out, and re-sealing a deck. Give them their own page with a "last done" and a "next due" column.

Keep the records in one place

When something breaks, you want the model number, the warranty end date and the plumber's number in about thirty seconds. Keep one page for where the shutoffs are, one for contacts and account numbers, and one for appliances with serial numbers and purchase dates. Add a repair log so that "when did we last fix this?" has an answer.
