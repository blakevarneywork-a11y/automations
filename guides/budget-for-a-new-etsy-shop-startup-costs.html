What it really costs to open an Etsy shop (and how to budget for it)

Opening an Etsy shop looks cheap, and the first fee is small. The costs that surprise new sellers arrive later, one sale at a time. Put every cost on one list before you list your first item, and you can price with confidence.

One-time and per-listing costs

  • Listing fee: a small flat fee for each listing, and it renews when the item sells or the listing expires. Check the current amount on Etsy's fee page.
  • Setup fee: some regions charge one when you open the shop. Check yours.
  • Supplies and tools: materials, packaging, a label printer, photo props. Keep receipts. These are business expenses.

Costs taken from every sale

  • Transaction fee: a percentage of the item price, and often of the shipping you charge too.
  • Payment processing: a percentage plus a small flat amount per order.
  • Offsite ads: if Etsy brings the buyer through its own advertising, a further percentage applies.
  • Shipping: what the carrier actually charges, which is often more than you collected.

Look up each current rate on Etsy and write it down with the date. Rates change.

Work out profit per sale

For one item, add up the lot:

profit = price + shipping charged
         - materials - packaging - actual postage
         - transaction fee - processing fee - ads fee - listing fee

If the number is under what your time is worth, raise the price, cut a cost, or drop the product. Do this before you list, not after your tenth order.

Price for the margin you want

Work backward. To keep a target margin, the price has to cover your costs and also the fee percentages that grow with the price:

price = (costs + flat fees) / (1 - fee percentages - target margin)

Example with made-up numbers: costs and flat fees of $6, total fee percentage 12%, target margin 30%. Price = 6 / (1 - 0.12 - 0.30) = 6 / 0.58, about $10.34.

Keep it in a spreadsheet

A simple sheet with one row per product (price, materials, shipping, each fee rate) does all of this and updates when Etsy changes a rate. Keep a second tab for your running totals so you see the true shop profit each month, not just the sales figure.

This is a planning aid, not tax or accounting advice. Fee rates and rules vary by country and change over time.
